    What this follow-up answers

    The owner picked the stacked family's S1 geometry (Record centred above the field, field pinned to the very bottom) with a round Record, over S2–S4's spacing/order/scroll changes. This file does not revisit that geometry — it answers a different question: drawn how? All four options below share the exact same layout math (Record 72pt diameter, centred; field 44pt, full width, at the very bottom) and differ only in material: translucent vs. opaque, panel vs. free-floating.

    V-GLASS — Liquid Glass, the owner's first idea

    Round Record and the field are two independent GlassView surfaces (expo-glass-effect, already a dependency — glassEffectStyle: 'regular', tintColor, isInteractive), floating free over profile-list, which runs to the screen's true bottom edge underneath them — the iMessage search-bar / Instagram tab-bar family the owner named. Approximated here with CSS backdrop-filter: blur(), the closest web analogue to a native glass surface.

    Principle: Jakob's law — this is the platform's own current default for bottom chrome (home-actions.md §5), so nothing here needs to be learned. Tint: Record is tinted with a proposed colors.glassTintMoss (moss @ ~0.84) rather than a neutral tint, so it keeps signalling "the primary one" (R2) even as translucent material — the field uses a neutral colors.glassTint (paper @ ~0.85). Edge: a thin colors.edge ring stays on both glass surfaces even though Apple's own glass rarely draws one — a deliberate accessibility choice, not a purity compromise: a blurred boundary alone cannot be guaranteed 3:1 against arbitrary content, and WCAG 1.4.11 is a hard rule here, not a trade. Worst case (toggle "content behind chrome" → bottom): scroll the list to its last four rows, redrawn with dark, photo-style avatars directly behind both glass surfaces — the actual stress case for "Record" and the placeholder staying legible. Reduce Transparency (toggle above): both surfaces drop the blur and render as flat moss/paper — identical to V-FLOATING below, which is literally this variant's fallback, not a separate thing to design twice. List inset: nothing reserves flex space for floating islands, so .content needs padding-bottom: 152px (Record 72 + gap 8 + field 44 + safe-area 20 + an 8pt buffer) so the last row and home-index clear the glass rather than sit behind it — approximate, confirm against the shipped components on device, R7's usual caveat. Keyboard up: Record hides (R8), the field island keeps its fixed bottom offset and simply renders in front of the keyboard graphic by stacking order, the same technique this project's other home-actions mocks already use.
    Contrast cannot be settled in a static mock. A live GlassView blurs and samples whatever is actually behind it; the tint opacities above (.84/.85) are a starting point, not a proven number. Before this could ship, the engineering check is: with the darkest realistic row (a full-bleed photo avatar) directly behind each surface, does placeholder text and the word "Record" still measure 4.5:1 on-device? If tuning the tint high enough to guarantee that makes the glass read as "basically paper," that is this variant telling us it doesn't have room to be very transparent on this content — worth knowing before investing in it, not a reason to skip the check.
    STYLE.md tension, named rather than resolved here. STYLE.md's one deliberate visual risk is the Briefing card's brass stripe — "everything else quiet and disciplined" — and home-actions.md already flagged a glass field as "the first glass in a paper-only system," needing its own STYLE.md line before it ships. That line doesn't exist yet. This is the owner's call to spend or not, not a reason this option is excluded here.
    Verdict: the most visually ambitious and the one the owner asked for by name — worth prototyping for real, but the only one of the four carrying genuine, unresolved accessibility and visual-discipline risk that this document can name but not close.

    V-FADE — Paper panel, the list dissolves (no blur)

    A fully opaque paper panel holds Record and the field, same as the original A1/S1 shape — but the last 32px of scrollable content, just above the panel, fades to solid paper through a plain CSS gradient mask (a LinearGradient mask in the real build, no blur anywhere). The panel itself is flat, undecorated paper.

    Principle: this is closest to STYLE's own "marginalia" grounding — content doesn't get clipped, it dissolves into the page the way handwriting trails off toward a margin. Contrast: zero new risk — every surface is fully opaque paper or moss, both already-tested pairings. Chrome height: an in-flow flex sibling, no floating-inset math: pad-top 8 + Record 72 + gap 8 + field 44 + pad-bottom (8 + 20 safe area) = 160pt reserved, computed from this mock's CSS. List inset: none needed beyond the existing flex layout — the panel reserves its own space, exactly like the previously-shipped A1 family, so home-index's existing offsets keep working unchanged. Keyboard up: same mechanism as A1 — Record's area hides, the field stays in the (now shorter) panel.
    Verdict: the lowest-risk, most on-brand of the four — no new accessibility question, no tokens beyond a shadow-free gradient mask, and a visual idea (dissolving rather than clipping) that extends STYLE's own grounding rather than importing a platform trend. Costs the most reserved chrome height of the three opaque options (160pt vs. notched's 77pt reserved).

    V-NOTCHED — Solid bar, Record on the hairline (shutter)

    A plain opaque paper bar with a 1px colors.line top hairline holds the field; round Record is positioned so its top half pokes above that hairline — the camera-shutter / elevated-notch read the brief named, fully opaque throughout.

    Principle: Von Restorff by actual elevation rather than colour or shape alone — Record is the one thing in this screen that visibly breaks the horizon line. Chrome height: the bar's own reserved flex height is just border(1) + pad-top(4) + field(44) + pad-bottom(8+20) = 77pt — the smallest reserved footprint of all four, because Record's overlap is drawn on top of content rather than inside the bar's own box. Overlap risk, named plainly: that 36pt of overlap is drawn on top of whatever is rendered just above the bar — the same risk home-actions.md's Option C named for record-fab. Handled here with a 36pt content padding-bottom buffer so the overlap only ever falls on dead paper space, never a real row or home-index's tail — approximate, confirm on device. Contrast: zero new risk, same as V-FADE. Shadow: a soft lift (proposed shadow.float) under Record only, so it reads as sitting slightly above the bar rather than embedded in it.
    Verdict: the best chrome-height result of the three opaque options and the most distinctive silhouette of the four overall at zero new contrast risk — the trade is a fixed 36pt overlap buffer to verify on device, a smaller, better-understood version of the same risk V-GLASS and V-FLOATING both carry at a larger scale.

    V-FLOATING — Free-floating, shadow only, no glass

    The same two floating islands as V-GLASS, but opaque — plain moss circle, plain paper pill, each lifted with a soft shadow (shadow.float) instead of a panel or blur. This is V-GLASS's own Reduce Transparency fallback, drawn here as its own option so it can be judged on its own merits too.

    Principle: Day One's floating create-button family (home-actions.md §5), minus any blur — the "floating" read without the glass risk. Contrast: zero new risk, same opaque pairings as V-FADE/V-NOTCHED. List inset: identical math to V-GLASS — 152pt content padding-bottom, same device-verification caveat. Edge: the field keeps its edge ring even with a shadow added — a shadow alone isn't a reliable 3:1 boundary, so it is additive here, not a replacement.
    Verdict: all of V-GLASS's floating composition and none of its contrast risk, at the cost of V-GLASS's own visual distinctiveness — the safe twin, and worth keeping specified regardless of which other option ships, since it is the Reduce Transparency answer either way.

    Recommendation

    Ranked: V-NOTCHED, then V-FADE, then V-FLOATING, then V-GLASS. V-NOTCHED gives the smallest reserved chrome and the most distinctive, on-brand silhouette (a shape cue doing the work glass would otherwise do) at zero accessibility risk and only a small, well-understood overlap buffer to verify — it is the strongest beauty-to-risk ratio of the four. V-FADE is the safest and most "Andy" of the four (the dissolve reads as marginalia, not a platform import) if the owner would rather not have any overlap to verify at all, at the cost of the tallest reserved chrome. V-FLOATING is worth keeping specified regardless of the final pick, since it is V-GLASS's own Reduce Transparency answer. V-GLASS is the most visually striking and the one the owner reached for first — it is shown here in full, and is not ruled out — but it is the only one of the four whose central claim (legible text over a system blur over the darkest row this app will ever show) cannot be proven in a mock, only on a device, and it is the one STYLE.md flagged in advance as needing its own line before it spends the app's second deliberate visual risk. If the owner wants to pursue it, the next step is a real GlassView build against the actual worst-case row on a device, not a further round of mocks.

    Proposed tokens

    • colors.glassTint — paper (#E8E6DE) at ~0.85 opacity, the neutral glass tint (V-GLASS's field). Starting value only; the real alpha needs on-device tuning against the darkest realistic row before it's fixed.
    • colors.glassTintMoss — moss (#5C6B4F) at ~0.84 opacity, so a translucent Record still signals "primary" (R2). Same on-device caveat.
    • shadow.float — a single soft elevation shadow (offset 0/4, blur 14, ink @ ~0.22) for any control that floats free of a panel (V-NOTCHED's lift, V-FLOATING's islands). theme.ts has no shadow/elevation concept today — this would be the first, and needs its own STYLE.md line regardless of which material option ships, since V-NOTCHED and V-FLOATING both use it.

    STYLE.md change either glass token would need: a paragraph under "Color Tokens" naming them as a second, narrowly-scoped departure from flat opaque colour — after the Briefing card's brass stripe — confined to this one chrome, with the same "don't spread this treatment elsewhere" discipline brass already models.

    Names, if a variant ships

    • record-button--round — the round shape of the existing record-button (role unchanged: still navigates to record, still says "Record").
    • home-bottom-bar--glass / --fade / --notched / --floating — material states of the existing home-bottom-bar region (role, not look — naming rule 6 — even where V-GLASS/V-FLOATING render it as two separate floating pieces rather than one visual bar).
    • home-list-fade (new, V-FADE) — the decorative gradient mask.
    • home-search-field--glass (new, V-GLASS) — the glass-material state of home-search-field.

    Recording preview — hypothetical, not a build spec

    The "recording preview" toggle shows Record adopting the recording treatment record-flow.md's Final choice settled: a solid alert fill, a slowly pulsing dot (frozen under Reduce Motion), the word swapping to an elapsed time. Deliberately, in every material variant — including V-GLASS — this preview drops to a fully opaque alert fill, never a tinted-glass alert. Recording is the one state this app already always renders at full, unambiguous opacity (mic-button--listening today); this preview keeps that consistent rather than inventing a translucent alert. This is shown for visual-language consistency only — tapping Record on home still only ever navigates to record (home-actions.md R1); no new inline-recording behaviour on home is being proposed or built here.

    Accessibility, shared across all four

    • Targets: Record is 72×72pt (well over the 44pt floor, inside the brief's "≥64pt suggested" range) in every variant; the field stays 44pt tall.
    • Meaningful edges: every field keeps a colors.edge ring (3.2:1, WCAG 1.4.11) regardless of material — a blur or a shadow is additive, never the only boundary cue, in all four.
    • No colour-only states: Record always carries the word "Record" (R1); the hypothetical recording state always pairs its colour with the word and the time, never colour alone.
    • VoiceOver order: list → home-search-field → home-ask-row → Record, in every variant — Record is drawn above the field in all four, so all four need the same explicit order override already named for the earlier "S1 oval" exploration. None of the four makes this better or worse than the others.
    • Dynamic Type: at the largest accessibility size (toggle above), round Record becomes a short stadium — same fallback in all four, converging toward the earlier S1-oval's own "grows to full width" precedent, so the word never clips.
    • Reduce Motion: the only new motion in any of the four is the hypothetical recording dot's pulse, which freezes under Reduce Motion (toggle above) — no variant here introduces a crossfade or transition beyond what the already-approved A1/S-family already specifies for hiding Record while typing.
    • Reduce Transparency: only V-GLASS is affected; its fallback is pixel-identical to V-FLOATING (toggle "reduce transparency" with material set to Glass to see it live).
