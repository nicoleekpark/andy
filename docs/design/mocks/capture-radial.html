What this follow-up answers

The owner wants the feel of a dimmed-focus arc/fan menu around the already-settled round record-button, not a return to either reference wholesale. All three variants below share the same arc geometry (two labelled satellites, Write and Photo, fanning upward from Record's centre, in the thumb zone) and the same downstream screens (record--typing, the photo sheet, the offline notice — unchanged from capture-doors.html, shown briefly here for continuity). They differ only in when the arc is visible and what a tap on Record means — the same axis capture-doors.md's M1/M2/M3 already varied, redrawn as an arc instead of a scrim sheet.

Geometry, shared by all three

Each satellite sits at radius ~104pt from Record's centre, ~50° off vertical: dx ≈ ±80pt, dy ≈ -67pt (toggle "geometry overlay" to see the dashed radius circle and the offsets live). At 390pt width, with Record centred at x=195: the left satellite's left edge lands at ~49pt from the screen edge, the right satellite's right edge at ~341pt — both comfortably inside the 16pt margin on either side (space.lg), with room to spare. Approximate, confirm on device — the usual caveat this project's geometry mocks carry, same as home-actions.md's own overlap-buffer math.

One-handed reach (toggle "hand" above): the arc is bilaterally symmetric around a centred Record, so it is identical for a right- or left-handed grip — nothing here favours one thumb's arc the way a corner-anchored FAB (home-actions.md Option C) would. The toggle doesn't change the layout; that is the finding.

R1 — Tap to fan

Tap Record: a scrim dims the list, the arc opens (Write, Photo), and Record itself stays exactly where it is, growing slightly (record-button--armed, 72pt → 84pt) — never relabelled, never replaced by a ✕ the way Google Calendar's own FAB does. Tap the now-larger Record again to start listening. Voice is 2 taps, zero finger travel — the thumb never leaves the one spot.

Principle: Von Restorff by motion — the arc visibly grows out from behind the one thing that doesn't move, which is also the thing most worth tapping twice in a row. The button-naming question (capture-doors.md R3), resolved differently from M1: M1 had to rename "Record" to "New note" because tapping it opened a menu containing a Record row — the button and the door became two different things. Here Record never stops being the one and only path to listening; Write and Photo are drawn beside it, not inside it, so the label stays literally true in a stricter sense than M1's. Worth seeing plainly, not declared settled: the first tap doesn't record — it arms. Whether "Record, tap twice" still satisfies "one action, one name" as cleanly as a button that simply does the thing on every tap is a smaller, real hair to split, and the owner should weigh it with eyes open rather than take R1's resolution as fully closing the question M1 raised.
Cost named plainly: voice costs a tap more than R2/R3 (2 vs 1) — the same cost M1 paid, for less of M1's "every door equal weight" benefit, since Write/Photo are still visually secondary satellites, not equal-weight peers.
Verdict: the closest match to the Google Calendar reference's exact gesture (tap opens, tap-again acts), with a genuinely better-kept label than M1 paid for it with — but it is dominated by R2 below on both the tap count and the "no gesture required" question, for a naming benefit R2 matches in full.

R2 — Tap records at once; hold fans

Tap Record: listening starts immediately, exactly as it does today, no detour (1 tap). A small, separately-labelled "More" badge (capture-arc-more, new) sits at Record's upper edge — reusing M2's already-approved words, "Write or add a photo", as its VoiceOver label, so nothing new is invented there. Press-and-hold Record is a shortcut to the same arc — never the only way in, per capture-doors.md R4 and Apple HIG/NN/G's rule that a command must never live only behind a gesture nothing shows exists. A first-run hint (toggle above) points at the badge once, then stays dismissed.

Principle: recognition over recall — Record's behaviour never changes from what a returning user already knows (Fitts's law: it stays the largest, nearest, unchanged target); the one new thing to learn is a single small badge, discovered once via the hint and reachable by a plain tap forever after. Button-naming question: fully resolved, not just better-kept. Record always means "start listening," on every tap, with no exception and no "armed" middle state to reason about — the cleanest of the three on this specific question.
Named, same shape as M2's own open risk: the badge is a new, unlearned affordance, the same risk M2's capture-more-button already named for itself — here drawn smaller and closer to Record, which may read as more integrated (Air Command's own idiom) or may read as easier to miss; this is exactly what capture-doors.md's own TestFlight findability pass/kill line (§10) would settle, not something arguable from a mock alone.
Verdict: the strongest match to what the owner asked to see — the Air Command arc-and-labels feel, kept alive by a real press-and-hold — while matching M2/M3's 1-tap voice and fully closing the naming question R1 only partly closes. The one real cost is a new, small, as-yet-unlearned control, named rather than hidden.

Why not icon-only?

The badge carries the single word "More" beside its chevron rather than being a bare icon — unlike settings-button's gearshape (icon-only, acceptable there because it is one universally-recognised symbol with a clear spoken label and nothing else competing for its meaning), a "more capture options" affordance has no equally-settled icon convention yet, so this mock keeps the word rather than relying on the chevron alone. Flagged for product-strategist: "More" is this designer's shortest-possible proposed word, not from the brief's copy table (C1–C15) — confirm or adjust before this ships.

R3 — Arc always half-open

Write and Photo rest, permanently, in the arc's final position beside Record — no scrim, no open state, nothing to reveal, ever. Tap Record: listening starts at once. Tap Write or Photo: that door opens at once. Every door in this variant is exactly one tap — the only one of the three (or of M1/M2/M3) where that is true of all three doors simultaneously.

Principle: Hick's law at its floor — there is no decision about whether to reveal anything, because nothing is ever hidden; and Jakob's law in reverse — this is the one variant with no gesture or control to learn at all, at the acknowledged cost of not delivering the "dimmed focus" drama either reference actually showed.
Named plainly: this is the one variant that doesn't deliver the thing the owner explicitly asked to explore — there is no scrim, no fan-out, no moment of focus. It is included because it is the honest zero-cost floor the other two should be judged against, not because it matches the brief of this follow-up.
Verdict: the fastest and simplest of the three by every tap-count and discoverability measure, and the one to pick only if the owner decides, having seen R1/R2, that the arc's visual drama isn't worth any new control at all.

Shared across all three

  • Downstream screens are unchanged. Write opens record--typing with the system Paste control (capture-doors.md's "Answered once"); Photo opens the same photo-source-dialog (Take a photo / Choose a photo); offline Photo shows the same photo-offline-notice text before any camera or picker — all shown briefly above, fully specified in capture-doors.html.
  • Offline, inline. Toggle "connection: offline" at rest — the Photo satellite's meta line swaps from "a card, a page of notes, a screenshot" to "Needs a connection" (quieter text, not a disabled control), so the state is visible before the tap, not only after it opens the full offline notice.
  • No new tokens. Every surface reuses moss, ink, edge, line, placeholder, scrim, paper and the one named alert exception (the recording indicator, already established by record-flow.md).

Accessibility, all three

  • Targets. Record is 72pt (84pt armed, R1); each satellite pill is ≥44pt tall; the close control and the "More" badge draw smaller (36pt) but sit inside a 44×44pt hit area, the same pattern nav-bar-action-right already uses for a small visual inside a larger target.
  • No icon-only doors. Write and Photo always show their word beside the icon (capture-doors.md §9) in every variant, including R3's permanently-visible satellites.
  • VoiceOver order, R1/R2 (modal while open): on open, focus moves to Record first (it remains live — tapping it again acts, unlike M1/M2's menu rows, which replace the trigger entirely), then Write, then Photo; the close control and the dismissed background are reached by the two-finger-Z escape gesture or a swipe past Photo, matching capture-doors.md R17. Toggle "voiceover order" above for the numbered badges live on the arc.
  • VoiceOver order, R3 (nothing modal, ever): the two satellites simply join the already-established bottom-chrome order — list → field → Write → Photo → Record (Record last, the same deliberate override home-actions.md's S4 exploration already established, kept here rather than re-opened).
  • Labels (proposed, confirm with product-strategist): Record — "Record a voice note" at rest, with an accessibility hint "Double dash tap again to start listening" only while armed (R1); Write — "Write it instead"; Photo — "Add a photo of a note or card"; Close — "Close"; R2's badge — "Write or add a photo" (M2's own words, reused).
  • Reduce Motion (toggle above): the fan-out and the close control's pop both become an instant position with only a 120ms opacity crossfade — no translate, no scale, no spring, matching capture-doors.md §9's rule for capture-menu.
  • Dynamic Type (toggle above): at the largest accessibility size, the arc itself is hidden and capture-menu--arc converges to the plain stacked capture-menu card it is a state of — full-width Write and Photo rows, no absolute geometry to collide — the same convergence pattern home-actions.md's S1-oval already established ("grows to full width" rather than maintaining a shape that can't scale).
  • Contrast. No new pairings: satellite pills are ink text on paper with an edge ring (3.2:1, WCAG 1.4.11), identical to every other field/control in this system.

Recommendation

Ranked: R2, then R3, then R1. R2 is the only one of the three that gets everything at once: 1-tap voice, the arc/fan feel the owner actually asked to see (on a hold, or a plain tap on "More"), and a fully — not just more cleanly — resolved answer to "can it still be called Record." Its one real cost, a small new badge to learn, is the same shape of risk M2 already named for itself in capture-doors.md, not a new kind of risk this exploration introduces. R3 is the right fallback if, having seen R1/R2, the owner decides no amount of visual drama is worth even one small new control — it is strictly faster on every door than either R1 or R2 (and than M1/M2/M3), at the cost of being the one variant that doesn't actually deliver a "dimmed focus" moment at all, which is the thing this whole follow-up set out to explore. R1 is ranked last: it is dominated by R2 on both the tap count and the "does a plain tap always exist" question, for a naming benefit R2 matches in full — it is the variant closest to the Google Calendar reference's literal gesture, which is worth seeing in full for that reason, but not the one to build first.

Hypotheses, not tested behaviour (same discipline as every prior exploration in this file's family): that a small badge beside Record is noticed when needed and ignored otherwise (R2) — tested directly by capture-doors.md §10's TestFlight findability pass/kill line. That the naming distinction this document draws between R1's "armed" state and R2/R3's always-honest label actually matters to a real user, rather than only to this analysis — nothing here measures that; it is named as a risk to watch, not a proven cost.

Names, if a variant ships

  • capture-menu--arc (new state of the existing capture-menu) — the arc-shaped presentation, used by R1 and R2; converges to the plain stacked capture-menu at the largest Dynamic Type size. Holds the already-named capture-menu-item--write / --photo and capture-menu-close.
  • record-button--armed (new state of record-button--round, R1 only) — the enlarged centre state between the arming tap and the tap that starts listening.
  • capture-arc-more (new, R2 only) — the small, always visible "More" badge beside Record; opens capture-menu--arc on a tap, same as a long-press shortcut on Record itself.
  • capture-arc-hint (new, R2 only) — the one-time tooltip pointing at capture-arc-more on first run.
  • capture-satellite-button--write / --photo (new, R3 only) — the permanently visible, no-indirection doors beside Record. Unlike capture-menu-item--write/--photo, these are never inside a menu and never animate open or closed.

No new tokens proposed by this document.
